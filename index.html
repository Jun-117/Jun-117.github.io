Questions about your kitchen

We rent our home. Can we still get it?+

Yes. The unit normally stands on the floor or a shelf beside your sink — no drilling. If you'd rather have it on the wall, that needs your landlord's okay, and if we ever take the unit back we fill the holes.

Our water comes from a rooftop tank and the pressure is low. Will it work?+

The unit has its own pump built in, so it does not rely on the pressure from your tank the way many purifiers do. We still measure your water pressure as soon as we arrive on install day — before anything is fitted or signed. If the flow in your kitchen would be slow, we tell you then, and you decide.

What happens when the power goes out?+

The purifier stops. Its pump and its UV lamp both run on electricity, and there is no storage tank — that is the price of never drinking water that has been sitting. So keep a jug or two filled, the way many kitchens here already do. When the power is back, open the tap and it works again.

What if something breaks or tastes wrong?+

Tell us and we come. It's our equipment — repairs and filter changes are part of the service, and we check the unit and log your water readings at every visit anyway.

Questions about the money

Is it cheaper than jar water?+

For many homes, no — and we won't pretend otherwise. If your family goes through about five 20-litre jars a week or more, KORA costs about the same or less than you pay now. If you use two or three, you would pay more than you do today. What you'd be paying for is never carrying a jar, never running out, and water that is looked after on a schedule.

Is there a contract?+

Yes — a written service agreement with a minimum term of 36 months. It says what you pay, what we owe you, and what happens to your deposit: leave early and the part you've paid is not returned; stay to the end and it comes back when the unit is returned in working order. You get the full text before you decide, and you keep your own signed copy. Read it first; sign only if you're comfortable.

Will my monthly fee go up later?+

Our plan is no. The amounts for all 36 months are written into your agreement, and if prices ever rise, we intend that to apply to new customers only — homes that joined at launch keep paying Rs 1,100 when they renew. The exact wording is with our lawyer now; what your signed agreement says is what counts.

How do I pay each month?+

Fonepay QR, Khalti, eSewa or cash — whichever is easiest for your family.

How small is 0.01 micron?

The green shape is one bacterium, about 1 micron wide. The dot inside the ring is one hole in the stage-04 membrane, drawn at the same scale. Water gets through. The bacterium does not fit.

A human hair is about 70 microns across — at this scale it would be seventy times wider than the bacterium.

Do I have to buy the purifier?+

No — that's the point. The machine stays ours, always. Filter changes, repairs and our care visits are all inside your monthly subscription. If it's not working, it's our problem, not yours.

How much does it cost?+

Rs 4,900 on installation day — that covers the installation and your first month. Then Rs 1,400 a month for months 2 to 13 (Rs 1,100 subscription + Rs 300 towards a refundable Rs 3,600 deposit), and Rs 1,100 a month from month 14. Filters, repairs and care visits are included, VAT is included, and there is no machine to buy. The same figures go into your written agreement.

What happens after I join the launch list?+

We message you when we are installing in your area. The first batch is small and the launch list is called first. Before anything is signed you get the agreement and every price in writing, and you are free to say no. There is nothing to pay to be on the list.

Will you test my water to show me it's dirty?+

No. We'll take a TDS reading if you ask, but we won't sell you with it: a TDS pen counts dissolved minerals, not germs, and our membrane keeps the minerals in — so the number barely moves, and that is normal. (A big drop is what reverse-osmosis machines show, because they strip the minerals out too.) And we never do the "brown water" show — that colour comes from the metal rods rusting under electric current, not from your water. Proving safety is a laboratory's job: once our first units are installed, water from a real Pokhara kitchen — taken before and after the purifier — goes to an independent laboratory, and the report will be published on this page.

When and where do you launch?+

Pokhara — first installs begin this winter, after Tihar 2026. The first batch is small and we install a few homes at a time, so the launch list gets priority.

Other purifiers say 7 or 9 stages. Why five?+

Count what each stage stops, not how many there are. Many long stage-lists belong to reverse-osmosis machines: RO strips minerals out, so extra stages are added afterwards to put minerals and taste back — and RO sends part of your water down the drain. Pokhara's municipal water is naturally low in dissolved solids, so it doesn't need RO. Our five: two sediment stages, a coconut-shell carbon block for taste and smell, an ultrafiltration membrane for bacteria and parasites, and UV light as the final guard. No storage tank, no waste water.

Our jar water seems fine — why change?+

Maybe it is — but quality varies batch to batch, and you can't see it. With KORA you never lift a 20-kg jar again, never run out on a festival morning, and never wait for a delivery: the water is simply there, at your own tap.
